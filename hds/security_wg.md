Presentation of the SIEM system and definition 

At AVA6, we place threat monitoring at the heart of our advanced cybersecurity services through the implementation of a SIEM (Security Information and Event Management). Think of SIEM as the “control tower” of your IT security: it gathers, organizes, analyzes and stores all security information from your infrastructure in real time. Its main objective is to quickly detect suspicious activity, prevent attacks and ensure regulatory compliance. 

The Two Pillars of SIEM: SIM and SEM combine two essential functions: 

SIM (Security Information Management): Data Management and Archiving The SIM collects and securely archives all logs generated by your servers, network equipment, computers, firewalls and authentication tools. It is the guarantor of the traceability of your digital activity. 

SEM (Security Event Management): Real-time event analysis SEM is dedicated to the dynamic analysis of events as they occur. It looks for unusual or inconsistent sequences that could indicate an intrusion attempt, an ongoing compromise or a misconfiguration of your systems. 

Unified visibility for early detection: thanks to this dual capability of recording history and real-time analysis, the SIEM provides you with a complete, unified overview of the security status of your entire environment. Whether it concerns your endpoints, network traffic, user access, or activities linked to multi-factor authentication (MFA), everything is under surveillance. This allows for the prompt identification of abnormal behaviors, threat detections, or subtle signals which, when correlated, may reveal a potential attack scenario. 

For OneOrtho, AVA6 has deployed a SIEM based on WatchGuard ThreatSync technology. This solution is hosted in a SaaS (Software as a Service) model within an infrastructure that complies with the strictest international security standards. 

The WatchGuard ThreatSync platform collects data from various essential sources: 

EPDR agents (Endpoint Detection and Response) installed on servers. 

Logs from firewalls and network switches (via Network Detection and Response, NDR). 

Authenticated login attempts through the WatchGuard AuthPoint MFA solution. 

These raw data streams are then enriched, analyzed, and prioritized according to their level of criticality for better alert management. 

The SIEM incorporates artificial intelligence (AI) and machine learning engines. These advanced technologies make it possible to automatically detect behavioral anomalies, trigger relevant alerts, and even launch automated responses in the event of a detected incident. 

These sophisticated capabilities significantly reduce the time needed to detect a threat (MTTD - Mean Time To Detect) and to respond (MTTR - Mean Time To Respond). In addition, the historic, timestamped logs greatly facilitate investigations following an incident. 

The SIEM solution also ensures data retention in compliance with regulatory requirements, with log retention over a rolling 12-month period. This allows OneOrtho to easily comply with audits relating to GDPR, HDS (Health Data Hosting), or ISO 27001 standards. 

Finally, access to the collected data is strictly limited to authorized teams within our managed security services (RedSoc). This ensures the confidentiality and integrity of all sensitive information throughout the entire data flow. 

The SIEM data is located in the EMEA region (Europe Middle East and Africa), and WatchGuard reports an availability rate of 99.9%. 

The data transmitted to the SIEM is encrypted and protected against any alteration. It does not contain any directly identifiable personal information.

Zero-Trust Application  

The Zero Trust security model is based on the idea that nothing should ever be trusted by default, whether it's inside or outside the network. Instead of allowing access simply because a user is on the company network, Zero Trust always verifies who the user is, what device they are using, and whether they should have access to a specific resource. 

WatchGuard applies this model through a mix of tools like multi-factor authentication with AuthPoint, automatic application control that blocks unknown or suspicious software, and a centralized threat detection system called ThreatSync XDR. 

These tools work together to make sure only the right people can access the right things, at the right time. The system constantly checks user behavior, isolates any unusual activity, and responds automatically if something seems wrong, which helps reduce the time needed to detect and stop threats. This approach lowers the chances of cyberattacks spreading across the network and makes remote work safer. 

With WatchGuard, Zero Trust is simple to apply and doesn't require heavy technical knowledge, making it easier to protect both small and large environments. 

In our case, we fully comply with the Zero Trust policy, as no unknown or unapproved application is allowed to run or communicate within our environment.

Presentation of XDR attack detections 

At AVA6, XDR (eXtended Detection and Response) is the foundation of modern, proactive cybersecurity. Unlike traditional siloed approaches, XDR automatically observes and correlates security signals emitted by OneOrtho's entire digital environment, in order to identify sophisticated threats that are often invisible when analyzed in isolation. 

The solution deployed is based on the continuous collection of security events from several key IS layers: 

The servers, protected by WatchGuard Advanced EPDR, provide precise data on running processes, abnormal system behavior and malicious access attempts. 

The network infrastructure, via next-generation firewalls and NDR-equipped core switches, reports flow anomalies, scan detections and signs of network compromise. 

The multi-factor authentication system, in particular via MFA WatchGuard AuthPoint, detects abnormal connection attempts, mass errors and sensitive access from unusual locations. 

 

All this data is centralized in the WatchGuard ThreatSync platform, which draws on advanced artificial intelligence and machine learning engines to automatically detect suspicious or malicious behavior: unauthorized elevations of privilege, side connections, stealthy attempts to execute malicious code, etc. 

Each behavior deemed risky is then analyzed, scored by severity level, and integrated into a prioritized operational flow. This enables AVA6 security analysts (RedSoc team) to intervene rapidly: quarantining, blocking, automated remediation or in-depth investigation depending on the criticality detected. 

Thanks to this unified and automated approach, AVA6 provides OneOrtho with a responsive, threat-centered detection capability, fully aligned with the stringent security, traceability, and compliance requirements of the medical sector.

Full Encryption - Endpoint Data Protection 

WatchGuard’s Full Encryption module provides centralized management of disk encryption on endpoints, utilizing native technologies like BitLocker (Windows) and FileVault (macOS). Through the unified WatchGuard Cloud interface, administrators can deploy, monitor, and control encryption policies without the need for manual setup on individual machines. 

Encryption is anchored by hardware-level protection via the TPM (Trusted Platform Module), ensuring secure key storage and system integrity. Flexible authentication methods are supported (TPM-only, TPM + PIN, password, or USB key), allowing organizations to enforce their internal security policies effectively. Additionally, recovery key management is centralized, reducing the risk of data loss due to access issues. 

WatchGuard also supports automatic encryption for external drives (USB, external HDDs), with granular enforcement policies (encrypt used space only, force encryption, etc.), enhancing portable data protection.
